# 2.2 The Control Problem

Centralized systems concentrate both power and responsibility in the hands of a few operators. This structure creates operational efficiency but eliminates resilience.

When a single provider controls access, an administrative decision, outage, or policy change can disconnect millions of users instantly. Governments can compel these operators to block or filter information. Platforms can remove content or entire communities without due process.

The same fragility exists in financial systems. Custodial wallets, payment processors, and exchanges manage funds on behalf of users, but those users have no technical claim to their assets. Accounts can be frozen for compliance reasons, service interruptions, or geopolitical events. In several markets, access to digital payments has been suspended or restricted with little notice.

This dependency on centralized intermediaries undermines autonomy. It replaces distributed trust with institutional trust and turns access to essential services into a revocable privilege. While these systems achieve scale, they fail the test of resilience and neutrality that modern infrastructure demands.
