# 2.1 The Privacy Collapse

Personal information is now the default fuel of online services. Most applications gather data not to provide functionality but to sustain their business models. Even when content is encrypted, metadata such as sender and receiver identities, message frequency, IP addresses, and device fingerprints are stored and analyzed. This information can reconstruct behavioral patterns, personal networks, and habits with remarkable precision.

This constant extraction has normalized surveillance as a feature rather than an exception. The modern user cannot participate in digital life without leaving behind a detailed behavioral trail that can be monetized, analyzed, or shared with third parties.

Meanwhile, regulatory proposals such as the European “Chat Control” initiative aim to require scanning of private messages before encryption. Although intended for public safety, such measures introduce continuous surveillance into personal communication. Once implemented, these technical backdoors can be expanded or repurposed for broader monitoring.

The erosion of privacy is therefore not only a commercial issue but a systemic one. It undermines the foundations of trust, security, and personal freedom that the internet was supposed to guarantee.
