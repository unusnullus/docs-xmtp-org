# 1.3 Regulatory and social shifts

The regulatory and social environment for digital communication is shifting rapidly. Governments, international organizations, and advocacy groups are redefining the limits between privacy, surveillance, and public safety.

One of the most controversial initiatives is the proposed European “Chat Control Regulation”, intended to prevent and combat child sexual abuse. If implemented, this law would require messaging platforms to scan private messages, images, and videos before encryption to detect prohibited content.

Although the proposal remains under debate, it signals a broader trend: regulators are increasingly willing to weaken or bypass end-to-end encryption in the name of security. Law enforcement bodies such as Europol and national police agencies have called for “lawful access mechanisms” that would grant technical access to encrypted communications.

Civil society groups, privacy researchers, and the European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS) have warned that such mechanisms would fundamentally compromise digital trust, undermining confidentiality and the security of personal data for everyone.

The tension between regulatory control and individual privacy has become one of the defining issues of the digital age. The direction of policy indicates that privacy can no longer rely on legal guarantees alone - it must be enforced through technology itself.

This reality sets the foundation for privacy-focused, decentralized systems that preserve user rights through architecture rather than policy.
